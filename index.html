THE CONCEPT

Speakers that work around your monitor

Desktop speakers normally occupy valuable space on either side of a display. ClampSound is a tall, slim enclosure that clamps to the back edge of the desk and stands beside the monitor, so the desk surface stays clear and the drivers sit close to ear height.

Version 2 of the design is shaped by feedback from the r/diyaudio community. The front baffle stays only 7 cm wide, while the cabinet widens toward the back to gain volume. Mids and highs face the listener; the bass driver and passive radiators fire from the side, where there is room for them.

FRONT

Compact MTM

A 1" tweeter between two 2" midrange drivers on a 7 cm baffle, symmetric around ear height.

SIDE

Woofer + 2 passive radiators

A 4" woofer with two 4" passive radiators firing sideways, crossed over low enough that the bass is not directional.

SHAPE

Trapezoid cabinet

Narrow at the front, wider at the back: more volume and depth for the woofer, with the side drivers angled slightly forward.

ACTIVE

DSP crossover

Each driver group gets its own amplifier channel. Crossovers, EQ, and bass protection are tuned in software.

MOUNT

Desk clamp

A clamp on the back edge of the desk holds each speaker, with adjustable height and toe-in.

BUILD

3D printed

The enclosure is printed in two sections with a removable front baffle, so drivers can be swapped during testing.

PLANNED SPECIFICATIONS

What goes inside

These are design targets, not measured results. Everything below will be checked with real measurements and may change as testing goes on.

ITEM PLAN (PER SPEAKER)
Size 420 mm tall × 190 mm deep; 70 mm wide at the front, 120 mm at the back
Tweeter 1 × Dayton Audio ND25FA-4, 1" soft dome
Midrange 2 × Dayton Audio DMA58-8, 2" aluminum cone, in a sealed chamber of about 0.5 L
Woofer 1 × Dayton Audio ND105-4, 4" aluminum cone, side-firing
Passive radiators 2 × Dayton Audio ND105-PR, 4", side-firing, bass box of about 5 L
Crossovers Active DSP, Linkwitz–Riley 4th order; starting points around 200 Hz (woofer to mids) and 2.7 kHz (mids to tweeter)
Amplification 3 channels per speaker
Enclosure 3D printed in two glued sections; PETG for prototypes
Mounting Desk-edge clamp with height and toe-in adjustment

Why a low woofer crossover? Because the woofer fires sideways, it has to hand over to the front-facing mids before its output becomes directional. Two 2" mids in an MTM layout give enough headroom to reach down that low at desktop listening levels.

BUILD LOG

Current project status

UPDATE 02 — DESIGN V2 · NOVEMBER 2026

New cabinet shape, real drivers, and a test plan

Thanks to everyone on r/diyaudio who commented on the first concept. Your feedback on crossover frequency, toe-in, and mounting shaped most of these changes:

  • The woofer-to-mid crossover moves down to roughly 150–200 Hz, because a side-firing woofer has to hand over early.
  • Two 2" mids in an MTM layout replace the single mid, for more headroom at that low crossover.
  • The cabinet becomes a trapezoid: same 7 cm front, wider back, more bass volume.
  • The clamp design now includes adjustable height and 20–30° of toe-in.
  • The driver lineup is now all Dayton Audio, and the 3D model uses their real dimensions.

Next: build a small sealed test box to measure midrange distortion at different crossover points, simulate the woofer and passive radiators, then print the first test enclosures.

UPDATE 01 — CONCEPT STAGE
